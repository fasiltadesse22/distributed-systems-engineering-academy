# Lesson 1.1.8 — Terminal, Reversible and Irreversible Business Effects

**Cluster 1.1 · Chapter 2 · Master Roadmap V1 / Cluster 1.1 blueprint · Type C conceptual analysis**

**Engineering question:** When does “undo” mean a genuine reversal, and when must the business create a compensating action?

**Evidence boundary:** All payment, merchant, kitchen, notification, and cancellation scenarios below are explicitly **hypothetical**. The Food Ordering Platform at Java SHA 081d1f1a877772e41d7a30ce0052e9609c71f6b5 records only volatile order-request intake. It has no provider refund, actual restaurant decision, physical preparation, or notification integration. POL-01–POL-10 remain unresolved. A scenario that assumes a confirmed provider capture is **not** a description of an implemented platform feature.

## Orient — The charge that did not disappear

Imagine a customer's payment provider has really captured 800 units of currency for a food order. The restaurant subsequently rejects the order. A developer sees the order record marked PAID and helpfully restores the earlier UNPAID value, perhaps by rolling back a database transaction or updating a status column. A subsequent customer view now says “unpaid.” The provider's ledger, however, still contains an 800-unit capture. The customer's card statement may still show the debit. Replaying the order history no longer explains the external money movement. Nothing the developer did contacted the provider.

**The key counterexample is simple:** changing the platform's representation of a charge does not reverse the charge. If a customer-facing business commitment must be remedied, a separately authorized refund or other remedy may be required. A refund itself is not instantaneous erasure: requesting, accepting, executing and confirming a refund are different evidence milestones. The business may additionally need to account for fees or settlement delays.

Ask four independent questions before using the word *undo*: What changed? Who owned that effect? Was the effect observed outside the local transaction? What new facts would actually repair the outcome? The answers determine whether a local reversal is possible, a corrective amendment is needed, a compensating operation is justified, or human intervention is unavoidable.

This is the continuation of Lesson 1.1.6's independent lifecycles and Lesson 1.1.7's guarded decisions. A guard determines whether an action is eligible under an assumed state and policy. It does not turn a committed external effect into something that can be removed by reverting an object.

## Orient — Learning contract and explicit nonimplementation

By the end, distinguish terminal states from physically irreversible effects, classify correction/reversal/compensation, trace order/merchant/payment/kitchen facts independently, design a defensible before-and-after evidence history, and explain residual obligations when a lifecycle closes. You should be able to classify a fresh domain without importing a food-ordering policy uncritically.

Prerequisites: Lesson 1.1.6 (state machines versus workflows and the difference between independent state owners) and 1.1.7 (preconditions, transition guards, authority, no-op rejection, and candidate versus committed fact). The reader will use the words **fact**, **decision**, **command**, **commit**, **observation**, **obligation**, and **reconciliation** in their strict business-engineering sense.

The authoritative blueprint classifies this lesson **C**, not A: the work here is a complete analytical model and educational visualization. No payment provider, restaurant acceptance, fulfillment command, refund API, compensating scheduler, database transaction, or Saga is implemented. The real Java service continues to expose local request intake only. The absence of a real participant is not a secretly successful integration.

Our analysis uses explicit teaching assumptions to show how policy alternatives differ; it does not approve a business policy. The product, commercial, merchant, finance, compliance, and operations authorities responsible for POL-01–POL-10 still need to decide the actual rules. If the imagined transaction requires an unapproved rule to proceed, the honest operational answer is **policy unresolved**.

## Understand — State closure is not effect erasure

A **state** summarizes an entity's position in a particular lifecycle, whereas an **effect** is a change that occurred in some owner-controlled domain. For example, a platform may move a local order projection from AWAITING_PAYMENT to CANCELLED. Independently, a provider may have captured funds, a kitchen may have consumed ingredients, and a notification channel may have delivered a message. A state update can be syntactically correct and still leave those effects untouched.

There are at least three distinct time axes: the time the physical or financial effect happened, the time the authoritative source committed it, and the time our application learned about it. In a real asynchronous integration these need not be equal or even observed in occurrence order. Restoring an earlier application snapshot rewrites one observation, not the source's past.

**An inverse transition is not necessarily an inverse business operation.** Replacing a PAID label with UNPAID creates a misleading projection unless authoritative payment evidence supports the new statement. Refunding a captured amount is an additional money operation, potentially with a different identifier, authorization, fees, timing, dispute constraints, and settlement path. Even a full confirmed refund does not make the original capture historically false.

The correct goal is usually to **restore an acceptable business position**, not to pretend that past events did not happen. That position might require a refund, replacement, credit, explanation, loss allocation or a manual exception. The chosen remedy is a policy and authority question; the mechanism must preserve the evidence.

## Understand — Terminal state is local to a lifecycle

A lifecycle's **terminal state** is one from which its defined transition function allows no further transitions of that entity *under the current policy*. Terminality is relative to the identity, the transition vocabulary, and the rule set. A one-shot restaurant decision might model ACCEPTED and REJECTED as terminal choices. That does not mean a completed order can never be disputed or that a refund cannot be issued. Those may belong to different aggregates, lifecycles, or subsequent cases.

Define a transition system as (S, A, δ), with states S, commands A, and partial transition function δ(s,a), guarded by policy and authority. A terminal t has no applicable ordinary outgoing command in that model. It does **not** imply that no other system can observe, reconcile, dispute or compensate the effects associated with reaching t. Some domains permit explicitly authorized exceptional reopenings, but inventing a REOPEN arrow silently violates a one-shot state machine's invariants.

Distinguish **terminal for processing** from **terminal for evidence** and **terminal for financial obligations**. A rejected order may require no additional fulfillment decisions but still require financial reconciliation. A refund attempt might be terminal FAILED but the customer's entitlement remains unresolved. An audit record can remain valid indefinitely even after its corresponding workflow is closed.

The existing Java RestaurantDecisionLifecycle's one-shot model is **pure and illustrative**. Its ACCEPTED/REJECTED labels are not actual binding merchant decisions. The present Java order-request intake remains REQUESTED_AWAITING_RESTAURANT and the actual payment status NOT_STARTED; the model's lifecycle diagrams are not provider operations.

## Understand — Four operations often called undo

**Correction** amends an inaccurate record or interpretation. If an operator typed the wrong delivery note before any external action, a verified correction can change the currently authoritative value while retaining who/what/when/why. A correction is not a backdated claim that the wrong value never existed.

**Reversal** is a true inverse for an operation whose effects can still be undone *within the relevant authority boundary*. Abandoning a draft reservation that was never committed is often simply a no-op/discard, not a compensation. A transaction rollback before an external side effect commits can restore a local atomic database state, subject to actual transaction scope and isolation. A genuine reversal needs evidence that every claimed effect was within the reversible boundary.

**Compensation** is a new, forward-going business operation intended to offset, remedy or account for a prior committed effect. A refund compensates for a capture only to the extent allowed and successfully performed; it does not delete the capture. A restock or replacement shipment may compensate for an inventory/fulfillment error without unshipping the original parcel.

**Remediation/escalation** is necessary when no reliable machine-actionable inverse is authorized, when evidence is missing, or when physical/informational consequences cannot be eliminated. Support investigation, customer communication, manual approval, loss allocation, safety response, or legal/compliance handling may become the responsible next step.

The classification must use evidence and ownership rather than labels. “Cancel,” “refund,” “void,” “reverse,” and “rollback” are overloaded words: their actual meaning depends on provider contracts, commit points, and observed outcome.

## Understand — Reversibility depends on the effect boundary

Consider a transaction that performs two steps: update an order row and call an external payment service. If the database update later rolls back, its SQL changes may disappear. The external provider call is not part of the local database transaction unless a proven coordination mechanism exists. A local BEGIN/ROLLBACK boundary cannot guarantee the provider also rolls back. The provider may have accepted the operation even if the HTTP response was lost.

The **effect boundary** is the set of state changes covered by a specific, enforceable atomicity or reversal guarantee. Do not draw it around code merely because the calls appear in one method. An in-process Java method is not an atomic multi-owner business transaction. A message sent to an email system might be delivered long before the sender's database write is committed.

A useful decision test: (1) identify the effect and owning system, (2) identify the commitment/evidence point, (3) establish whether a true inverse is supported, (4) identify competing or concurrent actions, and (5) determine remaining obligations even after the inverse. If any of these is unknown, “undoable” is a hypothesis rather than a guarantee.

For local data, undo may still fail as a legitimate business action after another actor has relied on it. A previously approved merchant acceptance cannot be made nonexistent by rewriting metadata simply because the platform owns that row. Semantics and third-party reliance matter even when a database permits UPDATE.

## Understand — Separate order, merchant, payment and kitchen facts

Imagine Order O-41, Restaurant R-9 and PaymentAttempt P-12. The platform observes a customer request, a restaurant decision, a provider payment outcome, and kitchen progress through separate authorities. Those four streams must be independently reasoned about. A combined order status is a **projection** across them, not a universal source of truth.

The actual Java project currently has a real local OrderId and restaurant ownership on intake, a REQUESTED_AWAITING_RESTAURANT intake snapshot, a merchant PENDING indicator and payment NOT_STARTED. It models additional merchant/payment/preparation states as **pure illustrations**, not committed provider or kitchen facts. The hypothetical timeline below assumes future integrations that do not yet exist.

In the hypothetical case, the provider confirms a capture while the merchant rejects. The facts “captured” and “rejected” can both be true. The order might be terminal for ordinary fulfillment while financial compensation is pending. The projection should not force one exclusive global status that masks their coexistence; it should surface the customer/operations action and unresolved money.

Similarly, a kitchen can begin preparing before an app receives the start confirmation. A cancellation request arriving before that observation is not proof that preparation had not begun. Missing evidence must remain missing; if source chronology matters, seek authoritative event identifiers and timestamps, not client clock guesses.

## Understand — Compensation adds a fact instead of rewriting history

A **forward-only** view of remediation represents the original effect and the remedial effect independently. For a confirmed hypothetical provider charge C-1 of 800 units, record C-1 as captured. If policy and source authority permit, issue a refund operation F-1 referencing C-1. Record that F-1 was requested, and later, only on authoritative observation, that F-1 completed or failed. C-1 remains historically captured.

A robust audit explanation resembles: “Captured 800 on C-1 at source time T1; merchant rejected at T2; refund F-1 requested under approved reason at T3; provider confirmed refund of 800 at T4; remaining provider-confirmed refund obligation 0.” If the provider confirmation is missing, the final step must remain pending/unknown, not inferred from a successful API submission.

A compensating operation can fail or be partial. Therefore the application must track **obligation versus attempted remedy versus confirmed outcome**. A single boolean refunded=true does not encode partial refunds, retries, approval failures or pending settlement. Replaying the compensating intent as if it were a successful financial fact is another form of false closure.

A complete business remedy may include fees, customer disclosures, chargeback risk, merchant liability, and operational cleanup. Those liabilities are policy questions in POL-05/06 and contractual/legal domains, not defaults chosen by a sample simulator.

## Understand — Refund is not a negative capture

Payment vocabulary is unusually easy to misuse. **Authorization** may reserve spending capacity; **capture** creates a financial collection instruction/effect under the provider's model; **void** may cancel an eligible unsettled authorization or operation; **refund** generally creates a subsequent return-of-funds transaction for a captured amount; **reconciliation** establishes what the provider actually recorded. The exact semantics, intermediate statuses and finality depend on the provider and payment network.

For a hypothetical captured payment, writing amount=-800 to the order table does not debit the merchant and credit the customer. Issuing a refund request is likewise not proof of completion. Provider acceptance can mean queued, not settled. Confirmation must be classified at the correct level: request accepted; provider recorded refund success; and, if required, customer funds available are separate observations.

With a partial refund of 300 against an original capture of 800, the outstanding amount is not simply “the original payment undone.” The system must document currency, amounts, allocations, fees, uniqueness and source identifiers, and any later second partial refund. These are hypothetical accounting examples, not implemented ledger arithmetic.

This lesson does not choose whether rejected orders always receive full refunds, how fees are handled, or how long a refund can take. POL-05 (liability) and POL-06 (completion evidence) explicitly require the appropriate stakeholder decisions and provider contract. The teaching rule is epistemic: do not say “refunded” without source evidence for the precise claim.

## Understand — Physical and informational effects resist erasure

Kitchen preparation consumes time, ingredients and labor. Reverting an order state cannot unchop vegetables, recover spoiled food or restore the preparation opportunity cost. A cancellation after work begins might still be a legitimate customer entitlement under some policies, but it creates a compensation/loss-allocation problem rather than a simple data rollback.

An already delivered notification is similarly **informationally irreversible**: one can send a correction or retraction, but cannot reliably make the recipient unread the message, unforward it, or erase third-party logs. A command to “delete notification” may only remove a local record. If a customer received “Your payment succeeded” incorrectly, a later correction must acknowledge the original communication and the now-known truth.

Irreversibility is not binary. A food order may have portions not yet prepared, materials that can be restocked, a courier assignment that can be cancelled, and an already delivered message. Classify each effect separately. A substitute order or service credit may restore customer satisfaction without restoring physical resources. Environmental, contractual or legal consequences may remain even after monetary compensation.

The decision to allow exceptions after preparation depends on POL-03 and POL-04. An educational example is not an entitlement or an instruction to merchant operations.

## Understand — Cancellation eligibility is a policy decision

Separate **request authority** (the customer may ask to cancel), **decision authority** (who may approve it), **evidence authority** (who confirms merchant acceptance or kitchen work), and **remediation authority** (who can authorize compensation or financial operations). The ability to invoke an endpoint does not establish entitlement to its outcome.

Suppose policy candidate A allows cancellation until merchant acceptance; candidate B allows it until preparation starts; candidate C allows it later but with a fee or exception. The same fact history can yield different legitimate answers. A state machine should enforce a selected approved policy, not hide a commercial decision in a convenient conditional.

If a cancellation request races with a real merchant acceptance, the observed statuses may be stale. The guard used to decide eligibility must eventually be checked under an appropriate authoritative commit boundary; that later versioned concurrency mechanism is outside Lesson 1.1.8. Here the goal is to identify that a timing-dependent policy cannot be proven merely from a React timeline.

When policy is unknown, mark it **UNRESOLVED** and preserve the request and evidence for decision. Do not silently treat a simulated teaching rule as production authorization. Never promise a refund solely because a local CANCELLED label appears.

## Understand — Unknown outcomes require reconciliation, not guessing

A time-out means the caller did not receive a result in the allotted time. It does not prove the underlying effect failed. A provider could capture funds then lose the response. Retrying blindly could create a duplicate transaction unless the provider's specific idempotency and operation identity contract is proven.

The right conceptual state is **unknown / pending source reconciliation**. Record the intended operation identity, source reference if any, prior confirmed facts, attempted time, and the exact missing evidence. Query the authoritative source or handle an authenticated provider callback when available. Apply duplicate and out-of-order handling under an explicitly tested contract; do not invent that contract here.

For a refund request lost after provider acceptance, the obligation remains under investigation. Reissuing a new refund with no verified deduplication may double-compensate. Conversely, assuming successful refund could leave the customer uncompensated. Human payment operations may need to resolve a provider outage or mismatch before proceeding.

Reconciliation is not “make local state match the last event that arrived.” It compares identity, source provenance, chronology, amounts, and authoritative transitions, while preserving conflicting evidence and operator decisions. POL-07 defines the eventual provider-unknown recovery rules and remains open.

## Understand — Correction preserves original and corrected evidence

Correction is appropriate when the recorded representation is inaccurate and the actual source fact is known. If a support agent accidentally associated provider reference C-91 with the wrong order, fix the association after proving the true relationship, not by deleting the provider's charge history. Record the earlier association, corrected association, who authorized the change, and the supporting source evidence.

Different defects require different repair types. A typo in customer-visible spelling may need a normal record correction; an erroneous charge requires a provider-supported financial remedy; an incorrect merchant commitment may need a contractual/business resolution; a false notification may need a corrected communication. The common “just revert the state” instruction cannot distinguish them.

Avoid using a compensated status as proof that the original effect never happened. “Order cancelled after capture and refund confirmed” is information-rich; “order never paid” is false. In audit-intensive systems, truthful histories and customer-facing summaries may legitimately have different granularity, but must never contradict the underlying evidence.

A correction itself can be disputed. Treat evidence and permissions as first-class: what source showed the mistake, whose approval governs the correction, and whether the change requires a subsequent reconciliation check?

## Understand — Audit trail links identities, causes, times and sources

A useful compensation history needs more than chronological status strings. It identifies **OrderId**, **PaymentAttemptId**, provider capture/reference, refund-operation identity, merchant decision identity, source-owner, actor authorization, reason/policy version, amount/currency where applicable, causation/correlation IDs and independently supplied source timestamps. For actual production systems, the exact retention/PII constraints require separate policy.

A good audit answer can reconstruct: what we intended; what we requested; what the source actually confirmed; what our system believed; which observation changed our projection; which unresolved obligation remains; and what happened on each retry. Every transition that claims success should cite a fact at its authority boundary, not an educational CandidateIllustrative label.

A correlation ID such as the current Java X-Request-ID helps trace requests but is not a deduplication key, refund-operation ID or payment guarantee. The actual intake repository's putIfAbsent by OrderId is process-local; repeated valid POSTs may create distinct orders. Thus one cannot use its successful local create semantics to infer financial exactly-once compensation.

The required audit trail is an analytical design artifact for this lesson, not a durable event log feature. No such provider or kitchen history is stored by the current Java application.

## Understand — Closure and recovery are separate obligations

An order can be **closed for fulfillment** while open for support, reconciliation or refund. Distinguish process closure, legal/commercial claim closure, operational incident closure, and data-retention closure. They are not the same terminal flag. This is why lifecycle closure should not silently erase a separate pending refund obligation.

An obligation ledger answers: which action is owed, by whom, to whom, for what reason and amount, backed by which evidence, with what deadline and exception path? A refund pending provider confirmation remains an open obligation even if the restaurant has irrevocably rejected the order. Likewise, a kitchen loss claim can remain open after the customer outcome was resolved.

Closure should have explicit prerequisites: authoritative financial evidence if money is in scope; communication evidence if a customer promise is in scope; exception approval if policy requires human judgment; and a way to detect residual unresolved effects. A system that sets CLOSED automatically when status=CANCELLED may hide outstanding work.

We have deliberately not designed a durable ledger, scheduler or distributed transaction coordinator. That implementation requires new authority, persistence, and failure experiments in later lessons.

## Explore — A decision tree for reversal compensation and escalation

Use the following decision process for each **individual** observed or suspected effect, not for a whole order at once. First ask whether the effect actually happened. If not, stop or reject the command; if unknown, preserve uncertainty and reconcile with the owner. If confirmed, ask whether it can be authoritatively reversed within its true boundary. Only if the source guarantees the inverse may you call that a reversal. Otherwise ask whether a separately authorized compensating action exists; if yes, create and track that new obligation/outcome. If not, escalate for a human, contractual or safety remedy.

**Classify using evidence, not the desired status.** A local model that says CAPTURED without provider evidence is merely an assumption. A database rollback is strong evidence for transaction-local writes but not for a kitchen action. The absence of a refund response leaves the refund uncertain, not completed. The key stages in the diagram are observed effect, authority, inverse capability, compensation policy, and remaining obligation.

In the accompanying inspector, change both the effect scenario and the evidence available. Predict the branch before revealing it; then inspect the before/after fact ledger. Every result is a **deterministic classroom classification**, not an executed payment or kitchen action.

## Explore — Trace an unpaid draft and a captured payment

**Trace A: unpaid draft.** A customer composes an order but no business intake or payment command has committed. Abandoning that local draft can discard unsaved fields; no financial compensation is owed on the supplied assumptions. If the platform had already recorded an intake resource, deleting a local UI representation would not undo that recorded fact. The actual Java application records request intake, not a payment, so the illustrative draft is distinct from its current API.

**Trace B: captured payment.** Assume an actual provider confirmed capture C-81 for 800 units. A restaurant later rejects. Step 1: retain the provider capture fact. Step 2: retain rejection under its own merchant authority. Step 3: determine approved financial remedy (POL-05 unresolved). Step 4: if authorized, create distinct refund operation F-19 referencing C-81. Step 5: preserve F-19 as pending until provider evidence establishes its result. Step 6: after confirmation, present both the original charge and the compensating refund without claiming time travel.

Compare A and B: the former may have no committed external effect; the latter has one and demands explicit new action. A 201 response from the present Java POST /api/orders merely records an order-request resource, not C-81. The lab's capture is a hypothetical provider fact.

## Explore — Trace consumed ingredients and an already delivered notification

**Trace C: kitchen work.** Assume the merchant's kitchen has verified it started preparing order O-41, used ingredients and spent labor. A customer asks to cancel. The platform may be able to cancel future courier dispatch, but cannot reverse consumed resources. The correct answer must separate cancellation eligibility, physical effects, merchant reimbursement, customer charges, and notification. If POL-03/04 do not authorize a concrete resolution, preserve the claim for human/contractual decision rather than auto-pay.

**Trace D: notification delivered.** Assume an email provider confirms a customer was sent “Your order is accepted,” but authoritative merchant evidence later contradicts that message. Deleting a notification database row doesn't erase the delivered statement. A correction message can address the misinformation; it remains a *new* communication. The previous communication must stay in the incident narrative, along with possible customer reliance.

**Trace E: duplicate compensation attempt.** Assume a refund request timed out after reaching the provider. Issuing an identical-looking new request with a new operation ID may refund twice. The safe next conceptual step is reconcile the original operation's source identity and status before retrying under a proven idempotency contract. Never upgrade “request submitted” into “refund completed.”

The diagram should show two adjacent lanes: source facts that remain historically true and **new** remedial operations. It should never depict an arrow travelling backward to erase a confirmed charge, consumed ingredient or delivered message.

## Predict — Classify six effect histories before reveal

Work through these cases without reading the explanations first: (1) a never-saved draft is abandoned; (2) a database-only uncommitted write rolls back with no external call; (3) a provider-confirmed capture follows a merchant rejection; (4) kitchen work consumed ingredients before cancellation; (5) a notification was already delivered; (6) a provider refund response was lost after submission.

For each case, say: which fact is committed, whose evidence is needed, whether a true inverse exists, what compensation might be authorized, and whether the correct classification is **reverse**, **compensate**, **escalate** or **reconcile**. Include an explicit answer for policy uncertainty. A “correct” classification does not imply the unapproved compensating payment may actually be executed.

For case (1), discard is possible because no external or authoritative effect is assumed; do not call it a provider refund. For (2), rollback is limited to transaction-local effects. For (3), capture remains true and a distinct authorized refund might compensate. For (4), consumed resources cannot be unconsumed and a contractual remedy is needed. For (5), corrective communication may repair understanding but cannot unsend the old message. For (6), unknown result demands reconciliation before potentially duplicate operations.

The interactive practice asks you to choose a classification and reveal the causal reasoning. Change the case and the prediction resets; the exercise records no real payment or workflow outcome.

## Engineer — Design a conceptual effect and obligation ledger

Create a table with one row per **effect**, not one row per global order status. Fields to reason about: effect identity, owner, operation type, request identity, observed source status, evidence reference, occurred/observed times, reversible boundary, approval authority, obligation identity, remedial attempt, confirmed remedy and unresolved difference. Cross-link source identities without forging a shared global transaction.

Example rows for hypothetical O-41: C-81 capture 800, provider-confirmed; restaurant rejection M-6, merchant-confirmed; F-19 refund 800, requested but unconfirmed; K-11 preparation, not evidenced; N-4 acceptance email delivered. From those entries, you can say captured money remains a confirmed original fact; refund remains pending; restaurant rejection is a separate fact; kitchen work is UNKNOWN, not NOT_STARTED; misleading email needs correction.

An **obligation** is not itself an executed effect. Write “refund under policy review,” not “refund issued,” while approval is missing. A successful provider request acknowledgement is a fact about submission, not automatically a fact about funds returned. If a refund later partially succeeds, maintain remaining exposure rather than overwriting the original record.

This conceptual ledger supports design discussion and incident diagnosis but does not claim a persistent event store, accounting subsystem or Saga. The obligations and owners are examples only pending POL-03–POL-07 and POL-10 decisions.

## Verify — Design policy examples counterexamples and tests

Derive a table-driven conceptual test suite from the classifications. The positive case for local reversibility must assert **no external effect existed**. Its counterexample adds a source-confirmed side effect and proves the inverse claim no longer follows. For a captured-payment compensation case, the expected “after” ledger must retain capture and append a distinct refund request; until source confirmation, refund completion remains false or unknown. For consumed ingredients, assert that an order status rollback does not restore physical resources. For a delivered message, assert a correction is separate and the initial message remains historical.

Other negative tests: an unresolved policy must not yield automatic entitlement; lost provider response must not be interpreted as failure; repeated refund attempts without a proven operation identity must not be treated as idempotent; terminal merchant REJECTED must not be rewritten to PENDING to hide loss; and no React scenario may emit an authoritative provider success fact.

For each test distinguish **model prediction**, **source-owner fact**, **application commit**, **external observation**, and **failure experiment**. The academy's deterministic tests can validate its conceptual classification; only a future actual integration and executable fault harness could support a provider retry/deduplication/reconciliation guarantee.

A useful review challenge is to mutate one assumption at a time: remove source confirmation, change captured amount to a partial refund, introduce missing kitchen evidence, or remove policy approval. The classification should change for a reason you can articulate, not because a global status label was renamed.

## Diagnose — Database unpaid provider charged

Incident: support reports a customer was charged. The order record says UNPAID after a rollback. A team concludes that the database truth disproves the customer. That conclusion confuses local projection with independent source authority.

Investigate in order: (1) recover request/operation identities and correlation, (2) determine what actual provider operation was requested and any authenticated response, (3) query authoritative provider status/ledger for capture C-81, (4) examine order status history and any database rollback, (5) identify any merchant decision or cancellation fact, (6) determine outstanding approved refund or dispute obligations, (7) correct the platform projection with provenance, and (8) communicate carefully using exactly the evidence level observed. A provider timeout is not a negative capture fact.

If capture is confirmed, the charge remains a true past effect even when the local DB says UNPAID. If refund F-19 is requested but unconfirmed, do not promise completion. If provider records are unavailable, mark financial outcome unknown and route to reconciliation; do not create a new charge/refund blindly. A terminal order status does not close the money investigation.

This is a **diagnostic exercise**, not a claim that the current Java project has a persistent database, external payment adapter or real incident log. It currently uses volatile memory and no actual capture operation.

## Defend — A Staff reviewer proposes to reset the state

A reviewer says: “To simplify cancellation, set every status back to NEW, release reservations, remove the last event and compensate automatically.” Challenge the proposal by identifying authority, real effect boundary, pre-existing promises, lost evidence, duplicate compensation risk, policy ownership, and failure handling. Ask which effects were confirmed and by which participant; which inverse operations the owners actually expose; what happens if the compensating action fails; whether partial success is possible; and which customer statement is now defensible.

Offer a smaller design: preserve immutable observed original facts; evaluate cancellation and remedy eligibility against approved policy; represent each planned remedy as a distinct authorized operation; record source-confirmed outcomes and unresolved obligations; escalate outcomes that cannot be safely automated. Do not force distributed Sagas, outbox infrastructure or microservices simply to explain compensation. A robust single-deployable architecture can reason about business effects before choosing remote coordination.

Interview transfer: explain, without jargon, why a refund is a *new* transfer rather than a retroactive erasure of capture. Then explain to engineering peers why atomic local rollback does not cover an external provider, and why “terminal” is a scoped lifecycle property rather than global historical finality. State precisely which proposed mechanism has executable evidence today: in this platform, only local intake and pure illustrative models.

## Retain — Transfer to banking booking and inventory

**Banking:** a booked transfer debit is a historical ledger fact; a return/adjustment is separate and may require network rules, reconciliation and client disclosures. A UI balance correction is not necessarily an interbank payment reversal. **Hotel booking:** abandoning an unconfirmed room-selection draft differs from cancelling a guaranteed reservation after a no-show fee becomes contractually due. Whether the fee may be waived is policy, not an ORM cascade. **Inventory/fulfillment:** rolling back inventory allocation before picking may be locally reversible; shipping a parcel requires a return, recall or reimbursement path, not deletion of SHIPPED.

For each foreign case, identify entities and owners; actual commitment evidence; one-shot/terminal transitions; reversible local operations; physical/financial/informational effects; explicit compensation actions; pending obligations; and escalation conditions. Draw two timelines: actual history that remains true and forward remedy facts that can change the present position. A correct cross-domain transfer must acknowledge local law/contract variation.

This lesson prepares you to study composing order, merchant and payment lifecycles in 1.1.9. It does not implement those lifecycles' actual cross-owner policy, provider interfaces or business authority today.
