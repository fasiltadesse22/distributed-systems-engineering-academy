# Lesson 1.1.9 — Ordering, Payment and Restaurant Lifecycle Composition

**Cluster 1.1 · Chapter 2 · Lesson 1.1.9 · Type A platform evolution and academy learning integration**

**Engineering question:** How do independent business decisions constrain one shared order journey without pretending that every participant has one universal state machine?

**Evidence and policy contract.** The Java Food Ordering Platform remains one Spring Boot deployable, with genuine process-local **order-request intake** and an opt-in, process-local **teaching composition**. It has no authenticated restaurant, bank/provider, kitchen or carrier integration. Everything labeled \`Simulated*\` is an explicit classroom observation, **not** externally authoritative merchant acceptance, capture, refund or fulfillment. POL-01–POL-10 remain **UNRESOLVED**; choosing a teaching alternative is not approval of a production marketplace rule. The academy's TypeScript mechanisms model causal relationships; the Java integration tests execute the local teaching implementation, not actual external operations.

## Orient — A successful order is not a single status

A customer submits an order request. The application returns HTTP 201. In the same hour a merchant might accept, a payment provider might not reply, a kitchen might start work, and a customer might request cancellation. There is no one authoritative participant that knows every effect at the same instant. Even if all logic is coded inside one Spring Boot process, business authority is distributed across *roles and real-world facts*. Calling the order simply \`SUCCESSFUL\` conceals which commitments are proven and which remain open.

Consider two apparently contradictory support incidents. **Accepted but unpaid:** a merchant acceptance is recorded, but no payment has been submitted. **Rejected but paid:** the provider confirmed a charge, and the merchant then rejected fulfillment. Neither combination is inherently impossible. Whether the marketplace allows either depends on the approved payment/merchant sequencing policy. Even when a particular sequence is forbidden *as a policy*, a real system can encounter it through late responses, external actions, races, or historical defects. Your model must be able to represent the observed truth and its remedies rather than force the world into an idealized happy path.

This lesson advances from 1.1.6 (separate state machines), 1.1.7 (guards, actor and evidence requirements), and 1.1.8 (terminality and forward compensation). We now compose these models in an **executable local application slice**, while refusing to invent the missing merchant or provider infrastructure. Learn to distinguish a simulated observation that changed a local teaching session from an authenticated source fact that would be required for a real order.

## Orient — Exact objectives, prerequisites and boundaries

The objective is not to memorize a long list of state labels. It is to derive a causal **decision system**: which commands are eligible from which combinations of order, merchant, payment, preparation, and cancellation state; whose authority and evidence are needed; what fails without any change; what happens under partial completion; and which financial obligations remain after fulfillment closes.

You should finish able to: (1) derive cross-lifecycle guard predicates from stakeholder policy alternatives; (2) implement a deterministic provider *simulator* behind an interface; (3) preserve independent typed identities and true intake status; (4) trace accepted-but-unpaid and rejected-but-paid cases; (5) distinguish requested cancellation/refund from approved or provider-confirmed results; (6) explain why local \`ConcurrentHashMap.compute\` does not establish a multi-service consistency guarantee; (7) reason about a lost response; and (8) defend what an actual remote version would require.

The real project evolution is narrowly justified by the authoritative blueprint: add a local teaching composition with restaurant-decision transitions and a deterministic payment-provider simulation interface. The existing UC-001 HTTP POST/GET remains *request intake only*. We do not add production merchant endpoints, an unauthenticated way to fake a payment capture, a kitchen integration, a database, a transactional outbox, a Saga or microservices. A different lesson owns modular decomposition and later infrastructure decisions. Tests must state whether they prove source validity, local processing, or something never implemented.

## Understand — Five independent views of one journey

At minimum separate these facts: **Order intake** belongs to the platform and answers “Was the request recorded?”; **restaurant decision** belongs to the merchant and answers “Was fulfillment accepted or rejected?”; **payment attempt** belongs to the provider's financial operation model and answers “Was authorization, capture, decline, or refund established?”; **preparation** belongs to kitchen operations and answers “Was work started or made ready?”; **journey orchestration** tracks a platform-facing request, cancellation, or simulated completion. An order's user-facing summary may combine all five, but must not replace their owners.

Use typed identities to avoid accidental equivalence. An \`OrderId\` identifies one recorded platform intake. A \`RestaurantId\` identifies its intended merchant. A \`PaymentAttemptId\` identifies an attempt, **not** a charge confirmation or an idempotency key accepted by a provider. Real capture and refund operations would ultimately require provider-defined reference IDs. A customer's repeated POST may record a second \`OrderId\`; current Java intake has no POL-08 customer-intent idempotency. A correlation header helps investigations but cannot ensure one money movement.

The conceptual state is a product: \`S = Order × Merchant × Payment × Preparation × Journey\`. Not every point in that Cartesian product is a permitted *new* transition. Nevertheless, observations can disagree, arrive late, or show a partially satisfied obligation. The model must represent that product without treating illegal hypothetical commands as impossible historical facts. A rejected-but-captured position is financially significant even when the business process has no valid route to delivery.

## Understand — Authority and evidence are not status strings

A customer has authority to **request** cancellation; that is not automatically authority to approve it. A merchant operator might have authority to accept a specific offered basket under contract, but a username alone does not establish identity, authorization or acceptance evidence. A payment provider may have the only authoritative evidence of capture. A kitchen system or accountable operator may know when irreversible preparation began. The platform may own the composed projection and notification wording, not the underlying independent facts.

State \`CAPTURED\` in a local Java record is therefore ambiguous without source provenance. A real provider-confirmed capture requires authenticated provider/ledger evidence and an operation identity. A deliberate classroom preset \`SIMULATED_CONFIRMED\` is only a *teaching assumption*. Both may look similar on a diagram; they are different evidence classes. No React visualization and no JUnit assertion can turn a local payment double into a bank settlement.

An authority matrix should label each transition with actor, proof, policy and commit boundary. For example: \`MERCHANT_ACCEPT\` requires merchant identity, binding basket terms, an eligible pending decision and POL-02 approval. \`OBSERVE_CAPTURE\` requires an actual provider observation, financial correlation and provider contract. \`START_PREPARATION\` requires accepted merchant commitment, kitchen identity and the POL-04 work-start boundary. In this lesson these are **explicit assumptions** passed to an isolated learning service—not production authentication.

## Understand — The policy register is part of the execution semantics

POL-01 asks whether payment precedes merchant acceptance. Its candidate alternatives include **payment first**, **merchant first**, or different sequencing under different contractual conditions. The choice alters guards and exception recovery. Under a merchant-first teaching policy, acceptance may be modeled while payment remains NOT_STARTED. Under payment-first, merchant acceptance requires simulated capture. Neither choice is legitimate production behavior until a commercial authority settles POL-01.

POL-02 determines what makes a binding merchant decision. POL-03 and POL-04 determine the cancellation window and work-start consequences. POL-05 determines liability and remedy for a captured-but-rejected order. POL-06 determines what source evidence permits the claim “refund completed.” POL-07 governs ambiguous provider outcomes and safe retry/reconciliation. POL-08 governs repeated logical customer intent; POL-09 actual pickup/delivery proof; POL-10 conflicting projections and customer communications. These are not ten isolated checkboxes. Selecting capture-before-accept while leaving paid-and-rejected liability undefined creates an incomplete business design.

The new Java \`TeachingPolicy\` requires deliberate alternatives: \`PaymentSequence.UNRESOLVED\`, \`PAYMENT_FIRST\`, \`MERCHANT_FIRST\`, \`EITHER_FOR_EXPERIMENT\`; \`CancellationWindow.UNRESOLVED\`, \`BEFORE_ACCEPTANCE\`, \`BEFORE_PREPARATION\`; plus explicit hypothetical refund eligibility and capture-before-prep switches. All non-UNRESOLVED choices are labeled classroom hypotheses. A default unresolved scenario fails closed on decisions that would silently invent commercial approval.

## Understand — The product state machine and transition predicate

Formally, let the composed state be \`s=(o,m,p,k,j,π)\`, where \`o\` is intake, \`m\` merchant, \`p\` payment, \`k\` kitchen, \`j\` journey and \`π\` the selected teaching policy. Let a command \`a\` include actor, assumed source evidence and, when needed, a payment simulator preset. The transition relation is \`δ(s,a)=s'\` **only if all relevant local and cross-lifecycle guards pass**. Otherwise the outcome is a named rejection with \`s'=s\` and no new success fact.

This is not one enormous hand-written transition table with thousands of states. Each lifecycle retains its smaller legal transition function. The composition adds *cross-state predicates*. For example \`canStartPreparation\` depends on merchant ACCEPTED, journey ACTIVE, current kitchen stage NOT_STARTED, assumed kitchen authority and—under the selected hypothesis—payment CAPTURED. \`canRefund\` depends on simulated CAPTURED, a qualifying rejection/cancellation, explicit assumed refund entitlement and the state of the refund attempt.

Separate **candidate decision** from **commit**. Earlier Lesson 1.1.7 could evaluate a merchant guard with no storage. In Lesson 1.1.9 the local composition service commits a new immutable *simulation snapshot* into a process-local map. This establishes a genuine Java side effect inside the teaching session, but never an authoritative merchant acceptance or financial movement. Say exactly what committed: a local classroom observation, not a bank or merchant fact.

## Understand — The payment state machine is an attempt, not a receipt

The existing \`PaymentAttemptLifecycle\` has states \`NOT_STARTED → SUBMITTED_UNKNOWN → AUTHORIZED / DECLINED / CAPTURED\`; a captured attempt may transition to \`REFUND_PENDING → REFUNDED\` under a modeled response. It is a pure model. Lesson 1.1.9 composes its transitions with a local port, \`SimulatedPaymentPort\`, whose deterministic adapter maps selected teaching presets to \`SIMULATED_CONFIRMED\`, \`SIMULATED_DECLINED\` or \`UNKNOWN\`. The adapter performs **no external I/O** and does not authenticate source evidence.

Observe how \`SUBMIT_PAYMENT\` does not imply capture. It moves the local attempt to \`SUBMITTED_UNKNOWN\`; a second command represents observing the simulator result. If the preset is RESPONSE_LOST, the observed state must remain UNKNOWN. The instructor may choose a later observation to continue that same local attempt, but the lesson does not implement a real provider's query API or retry identity protocol. The ambiguous outcome illustrates the need for reconciliation, not a safe-to-retry guarantee.

A refund request also does not mean money returned. From CAPTURED, eligible \`REQUEST_REFUND\` produces \`REFUND_PENDING\`. The simulator may confirm, decline or lose a response. Only a simulated confirmation moves to modeled REFUNDED, and the label explicitly says \`NotBankSettlement\`. In a real financial system there may be further provider and bank settlement milestones. Never label this classroom \`REFUNDED\` as customer funds restored.

## Understand — Merchant decision is independent and one-shot

The existing \`RestaurantDecisionLifecycle\` represents \`PENDING → ACCEPTED\` or \`PENDING → REJECTED\`, with either terminal in its small illustrative model. Lesson 1.1.7 wraps it in eleven guard checks: matching order and merchant IDs; actor role and actor scope; assumed technical authentication and business authority; expected state; still PENDING; matching basket terms; assumed merchant evidence; and the explicitly educational POL-02 teaching choice.

The new local service reuses that **guarded candidate evaluator** before committing a classroom snapshot. If a second \`MERCHANT_REJECT\` follows \`MERCHANT_ACCEPT\`, the underlying state is already terminal, so the second action must return \`Rejected\` with the *identical* previous snapshot and no new fact. A concurrent ACCEPT/REJECT race is resolved by the process-local per-key map operation, not by a real merchant system. This distinction matters: a correct single-JVM race test cannot prove that two external merchant clients or two application instances will observe one binding outcome.

Accepted-but-unpaid becomes inspectable: choose merchant-first classroom sequencing; assume merchant-operator authority and a source response; transition merchant to ACCEPTED while payment remains NOT_STARTED. This is a **valid scenario under selected teaching assumptions**, not a promise that the real commercial platform should take an unpaid order.

## Understand — Accepted but unpaid is a first-class partial state

Construct hypothetical order O-71, restaurant R-11 and payment attempt P-6. The platform has recorded intake. A merchant-first policy is selected for the classroom. The simulated merchant accepts. The correct combined state is: intake REQUESTED; merchant ACCEPTED; payment NOT_STARTED; kitchen NOT_STARTED; journey ACTIVE. No money is owed solely because the local merchant teaching flag changed; whether a binding merchant commitment exists is still unresolved outside the simulator.

The consequences are operationally important. If payment then fails, the merchant might have reserved capacity or purchased ingredients. Should the acceptance automatically lapse? Should the customer receive another opportunity to pay? Is the merchant permitted to prepare before capture? Those are business choices, not technical truths that can be invented inside \`if\` statements. A payment-first alternative would block merchant acceptance before the simulated capture and therefore change the reachable *intended* state graph.

Trace the anti-pattern: \`if merchant==ACCEPTED then order=CONFIRMED\`. If a customer interprets CONFIRMED as both fulfillment acceptance and secured payment, that projection makes a false compound claim. Prefer decomposed evidence: “restaurant accepted” (subject to actual source proof) and “payment pending” separately. In the current project neither of those external milestones actually occurs.

## Understand — Rejected but paid requires a distinct financial obligation

Now choose \`EITHER_FOR_EXPERIMENT\`. Submit a simulated payment and observe simulated capture. Only afterward record a simulated merchant rejection. The state becomes merchant REJECTED and payment CAPTURED. A single “order rejected” label hides the money; a single “paid order” label hides the merchant refusal. Both facts must coexist.

Under an explicitly assumed POL-05 refund-entitlement rule, the platform may propose a refund operation, moving the local payment model to REFUND_PENDING. If the simulated provider response is lost, the refund is still pending. If the local double later reports confirmed, the *teaching attempt* becomes REFUNDED. The original simulated capture is not deleted from the history. This models the 1.1.8 principle that compensation is a new forward operation, not rollback of a previous effect.

Do not let a developer “fix” the contradiction by changing CAPTURED back to NOT_STARTED. That would turn an observed charge into an inaccurate projection. Conversely, never initiate a real refund without a provider reference, finance authorization and POL-05/06 evidence. The new Java service permits only local hypothetical observations with explicit markers, so support incidents involving actual card charges remain outside its executable guarantee.

## Understand — Cancellation request is not cancellation approval

A customer click creates *intent*. It can be recorded even when permission to cancel is undecided. In the classroom composition, \`REQUEST_CANCELLATION\` records a separate \`CANCELLATION_REQUESTED\` journey state. This is not \`CANCELLED\` and has no financial effect. The next decision requires an assumed platform-policy authority and a selected cancellation window.

Compare two hypothetical gates. BEFORE_ACCEPTANCE denies automatic approval after simulated merchant ACCEPTED. BEFORE_PREPARATION allows the window to extend through acceptance but ends when simulated kitchen work begins. If the selected window is UNRESOLVED, approval fails closed. A request can remain outstanding and require an explicit denial or external policy resolution. A later code path must not silently treat the mere existence of a request as approved cancellation.

In this local teaching model, pending cancellation blocks new payment and preparation/merchant actions. This is a conservative *teaching conflict gate*, not a guarantee that a remote provider cannot capture after cancellation request. Real payment callbacks may arrive after a cancellation. Their authoritative historical effects must be reconciled, not dropped because the journey was already marked cancelled.

## Understand — Preparation and irreversible work change eligibility

A merchant acceptance is not kitchen preparation. The kitchen stage begins NOT_STARTED and can evolve to PREPARING and READY only through modeled, role- and evidence-gated commands. A local \`PreparationGuardProbe\` checks order identity, an assumed kitchen operator, prior merchant ACCEPTED, assumed merchant evidence, current kitchen stage, and an explicitly educational POL-04 choice. The composed service additionally requires journey ACTIVE and may require simulated payment CAPTURED depending on the chosen policy hypothesis.

If the customer requests cancellation while kitchen work is PREPARING, a BEFORE_PREPARATION rule blocks automatic approval. The request remains recorded. The simulator can then record a distinct denial, rather than falsely claiming ingredients were unconsumed. Real-world food, labor and spoilage require contractual rules and liability allocation, which this lesson intentionally leaves unresolved.

Even if the simulator moves preparation to READY, no meal was actually prepared. A successful local transition is only a demonstration of guards. The label \`SimulatedKitchenReadyNotReal\` makes this evidence category explicit. This is important for future extraction: physical work becomes irreversible outside the application's transaction boundary, whether the software is monolithic or distributed.

## Understand — Completion must not conceal missing delivery evidence

A composed happy-path teaching scenario can require simulated merchant ACCEPTED, payment CAPTURED, kitchen READY and journey ACTIVE before marking \`COMPLETED_SIMULATED\`. That transition demonstrates a conjunction of preconditions. It does **not** establish carrier pickup, delivery, customer receipt or invoice settlement; those are separate authorities and POL-09 remains open.

Ask what “complete” means in any system design. Order intake complete? Merchant acceptance complete? Preparation ready? Payment provider captured? Delivery complete? Financially reconciled? Each has a different owner and proof. A future production workflow may only close after actual handoff or delivery evidence. A demo that stops at READY must not present itself as a delivered order.

The local terminal journey also does not extinguish separate financial obligations. An order can be cancelled while its payment is still captured, and its refund can remain pending. Treat completion and money reconciliation as orthogonal concerns. This prevents a terminal fulfillment flag from erasing a financial exception.

## Understand — Policy selection changes the reachable paths

Imagine the same three independent lifecycle graphs. Select PAYMENT_FIRST: \`MERCHANT_ACCEPT\` is blocked until payment is hypothetically CAPTURED. Select MERCHANT_FIRST: payment submission is blocked until merchant is hypothetically ACCEPTED. Select EITHER_FOR_EXPERIMENT: both orderings are permitted inside an explicitly labeled educational scenario. Select UNRESOLVED: state-changing decisions requiring sequencing approval fail closed.

A “policy” in this model is a frozen parameter captured at session initialization. Attempting to reopen the same session with a different policy is rejected; otherwise a learner could explain contradictory outcomes by silently changing rules halfway through a trace. A real system needs versioned policies and a deliberate migration/retroactivity decision as well. Here the simulation demonstrates why rules should not mutate invisibly during a business journey.

The synchronized lifecycle inspector below lets you compare the same command under different stated assumptions. Notice that some cross-lifecycle guards turn red while unaffected independent state remains unchanged. This is a *state-space exploration aid* rather than an engine that executes financial effects.

## Understand — The local commit boundary is narrower than the business boundary

The application service owns a \`ConcurrentHashMap<OrderId,Snapshot>\` for teaching sessions. It uses \`compute\` to serialize a transition by the same OrderId in one running JVM. A rejected action returns the exact same immutable snapshot reference and does not append a success event. An accepted action constructs a new immutable snapshot with one extra \`Simulated*\` observation. The canonical \`OrderRepository\` record is never replaced, and GET /api/orders still reports PENDING and NOT_STARTED.

This is a genuine application-level write to volatile learning state, not merely a pure Java function. That makes the new JUnit integration tests stronger than a browser simulation for the **local** mechanism: they can assert actual Spring wiring, real order-intake prerequisites, per-session atomicity, and no-op rejection. However, no durability follows from a map, and no distributed transaction spans a bank or restaurant merely because the simulator is used inside one service.

A failure after an external provider call but before local storage would introduce an unknown effect with reconciliation requirements. The local deterministic adapter has no such external call. Its RESPONSE_LOST setting is a controlled *model of uncertainty*, not an experimentally observed network partition. Once a real provider integration exists, additional failure injection and real source evidence will be mandatory before promising retries or exactly-once behavior.

## Understand — Immutable histories and source classifications

A useful operational timeline distinguishes **command attempted**, **decision rejected**, **local simulated observation written**, **actual provider confirmed**, and **externally observed final effect**. The current simulation implements only the third category for successful classroom transitions. It uses sequential local observation ordinal values and \`CLASSROOM_ASSUMPTION_NOT_EXTERNAL_FACT\`; it does not create bank references or durable event logs.

Why preserve individual entries? When merchant REJECTED and payment CAPTURED coexist, a support investigation must reconstruct which observation was recorded first, what assumptions were selected, whether a refund was requested, and whether the simulated outcome remains pending. If a response was lost, deleting the unknown entry would conceal why a later query is needed. If a refund is confirmed in the classroom, overwriting CAPTURED would erase the reason for compensation.

Auditability requires provenance, not simply a list of strings. A production version would need authenticated source identities, money amounts/currency and immutable provider operation IDs, timestamps with semantics, authorization proofs, policy version, durable storage, and access controls. Those features are not silently smuggled into this lesson.

## Explore — An accepted-but-unpaid trace from intake to exception

We begin with a real local HTTP order intake, which produces a new OrderId, no accepted merchant and no payment. A separate \`begin(orderId, MERCHANT_FIRST)\` starts the teaching session. A modelled merchant-operator command with explicitly assumed evidence passes eleven merchant candidate guards and is committed **only to the classroom snapshot**. Payment remains NOT_STARTED.

Now imagine the customer cannot pay. A full real marketplace must decide whether to expire merchant acceptance, release capacity, request a different payment method or compensate merchant work. This lesson's Java simulator does not invent those downstream commercial rules. It can show why initiation of payment is still a separate action and why the order projection must not claim a completed checkout.

## Explore — A rejected-but-paid trace with forward compensation

Start under EITHER_FOR_EXPERIMENT. Submit a simulated payment, then observe a local simulated capture. Merchant reject is committed separately. The partial snapshot now contains payment CAPTURED and merchant REJECTED. Its \`hasUnresolvedFinancialPosition()\` predicate is true, even though the journey is not eligible for ordinary happy-path completion.

Attempt a refund with \`refundEligibilityAssumed=false\`. The correct result is POL_05_UNRESOLVED, identical snapshot, unchanged history. RESPONSE_LOST leaves REFUND_PENDING unchanged and appends a simulated unknown observation; a later simulated confirmation moves to REFUNDED. ## Explore — Payment after cancellation and the limits of local ordering

Trace a customer cancellation request while the journey is ACTIVE. The session advances to CANCELLATION_REQUESTED. A new SUBMIT_PAYMENT command is rejected with \`CANCEL_RACE\`. The rejected decision does not alter the payment attempt or write another simulated success event. An assumed cancellation approval may move the journey to CANCELLED_SIMULATED only if an explicit policy cutoff permits it.

Now alter the chronology: suppose a real payment provider already captured funds but its callback arrived **after** the local cancellation request. A production system must not discard that late charge merely because its local new-payment guard rejects further submissions. An integration must reconcile a prior external operation's outcome independently of whether future operations are allowed.

The current local teaching session does not have a network channel or a provider's external ledger. It demonstrates one-JVM command sequencing and the *requirement* to model ambiguous provider effects; it does not solve remote race precedence, timeout/retry ordering or exactly-once settlement.

## Explore — Cancellation while kitchen work starts

Choose a hypothetical BEFORE_PREPARATION cancellation window. Arrange for the simulator to observe merchant ACCEPTED, payment CAPTURED, and kitchen PREPARING. A customer then requests cancellation. An attempted approval is rejected as POL_04_CUTOFF; the request remains pending. An illustrative denial may return the journey to ACTIVE, but the kitchen stage remains PREPARING.

Change the assumption: if preparation were still NOT_STARTED, the same policy candidate might permit simulated cancellation. That does not refund money automatically. If an existing simulated capture is present, the financial position remains unresolved until a separate authorized refund path is considered. This is the crucial separation between *entitlement to cancel* and *evidence of a financial remedy*.

In a real kitchen, observed PREPARING may lag behind work already begun. A stale local flag is not proof of absence of irreversible effects. Source chronology, merchant authority, contractual cutoff and concurrency control are required to make a production decision. The educational race is intentionally limited to a process-local session.

## Engineer — Trace the actual Spring application end to end

Read the real source starting at \`OrderController.place\`, which calls \`PlaceOrderService.place\`. The command is validated against the synthetic server-owned menu; the application calculates a demo quote, constructs a typed OrderId/CustomerId/RestaurantId intake, stores it with process-local create-only \`putIfAbsent\`, and only then returns \`OrderRequestRecorded\`. The HTTP response is 201 + Location; its authoritative domain meaning is *request recorded*. It does not initialize the teaching session or change merchant/payment status.

The new \`LocalCompositionSimulation\` depends on \`OrderRepository\` and \`SimulatedPaymentPort\`, not an HTTP merchant/payment client. It requires an existing recorded intake and creates a separately scoped, process-local Snapshot with its own typed PaymentAttemptId. It will reject an attempt to replace a session's frozen teaching policy. Its command handling checks cross-lifecycle conditions and delegates illustrative merchant/prep/payment state transitions to existing pure models before committing a new classroom snapshot.

A deliberate absence matters: there is no operational endpoint that accepts unauthenticated “merchant accepted” or “provider captured” signals. Such an endpoint would expose false authority. Existing API clients see unchanged intake-only JSON; the new composed workflow is exercised through local application integration tests and can be studied through the academy's React mechanisms.

## Engineer — A deterministic provider double and evidence contract

The \`SimulatedPaymentPort\` is an application boundary with input Effect (CAPTURE or REFUND) and a teaching Preset (CONFIRMED, DECLINED, RESPONSE_LOST), returning only \`SIMULATED_CONFIRMED\`, \`SIMULATED_DECLINED\` or \`UNKNOWN\`. The \`DeterministicLocalPaymentSimulator\` is a local adapter. It makes identical choices reproducible under test but cannot reproduce real network scheduling, merchant configuration or bank settlement.

Why an interface rather than directly assigning payment state? It surfaces a future dependency on a separate owner, makes uncertainty testable, keeps application logic independent of a chosen adapter, and makes the absence of external authority explicit. However, prematurely implementing an HTTP provider client, retry middleware, message bus, payment transaction ledger or mock “production” webhook would hide unresolved semantics. The port is intentionally named *SimulatedPaymentPort*, not *VerifiedPaymentGateway*.

A classroom response loss models the question: “What if the caller did not learn the outcome?” It does not prove which external effect happened, because none exists. Reconciliation would eventually query an authenticated provider operation identity; reissuing a new real capture or refund without idempotency evidence could duplicate money movement. The correct lesson-level claim is: **unknown stays unknown**.

## Engineer — Commit a session without mutating the order aggregate

The session repository uses a \`ConcurrentHashMap\` keyed by typed OrderId. Within \`advance\`, \`compute\` examines the existing immutable Snapshot and produces either a \`Rejected\` result or a new \`Applied\` Snapshot. A rejection returns the same instance reference, code and explanation, and does not append a simulated success fact. The map's atomic function provides a narrow one-key, one-JVM sequentialization property.

The original Order is not updated because the real merchant, provider and kitchen sources do not exist. This isolation also protects UC-001's historic invariants: no changed menu quote, identity, request intake, public response or API acknowledgment semantics. The payment attempt belongs to the teaching session and has its own ID; its state is not a real Order.paymentStatus value.

Question for a design review: Does the map provide crash consistency? No. Does it make a provider effect atomic with a database transaction? No. Does it demonstrate a single winning local merchant simulator outcome under a two-thread race? Yes, to the extent observed by a deterministic JUnit race using the same session in the same JVM. Exact scope is the difference between engineering evidence and marketing language.

The real Java Spring integration suite creates actual order intake records first and uses the local composition service. This establishes framework wiring and real volatile session changes. Existing MVC tests separately preserve HTTP 201/422, GET and the original PENDING/NOT_STARTED meanings. Together these form **local application evidence**, not external business proofs.

## Verify — Fault injection, races and unsupported guarantees

A correct failure test must inject a fault that matches the claimed guarantee. The local provider preset RESPONSE_LOST is an *illustrative missing-observation fault*: it should not assert that the bank captured money. The corresponding outcome must remain SUBMITTED_UNKNOWN for a capture or REFUND_PENDING for a refund. A later simulator observation is a new classroom fact, not automatically a safe real retry.

A concurrent merchant ACCEPT/REJECT test uses two threads against the same session key. Exactly one applied local teaching transition should be observed; the loser gets a rejected outcome and no additional success label. This is useful evidence for the local serialized map operation. It says nothing about cross-instance conflict, durable optimistic locking, remote merchant acceptance or webhook reordering.

The future reliability experiments are concrete: restart during a local write once durability is introduced; kill after a real provider effect and before a database commit; deliver duplicate/reordered callbacks; race cancellation against real acceptance; retry a lost payment response under the provider's actual idempotency contract; and reconcile divergent source projections. Until those mechanisms exist, their guarantees remain **NOT IMPLEMENTED**.

## Diagnose — “I was charged, but the restaurant rejected”

Begin by asking whose evidence establishes the charge. In the present teaching environment, \`CAPTURED\` means a deterministic local simulator reported a hypothetical success. It is not a receipt. If a real customer reports a charge, the application currently lacks the provider reference or ledger needed to verify that claim. Escalation to actual payment operations, not a local state rewrite, is required.

For a future real integration, construct a chronology: order-intake ID and basket; payment operation/request identity; provider capture or unknown result; merchant decision identity/source; cancellation request and cutoff; refund obligation eligibility; refund operation identity and actual provider status; customer-facing projection. Preserve both rejection and capture if both are independently confirmed. Do not discard late payment evidence after cancellation.

Before revealing the diagnosis, inspect three hypotheses: (1) no provider capture ever occurred, only a pending local attempt; (2) capture was confirmed and merchant rejection genuinely followed; (3) request timed out and capture outcome is unknown. The appropriate next actions differ: do not refund an unproven charge, do not silently ignore a confirmed charge, and do not guess when source evidence is missing.

## Defend — Staff review of orchestration and premature distribution

A reviewer suggests three microservices, a Saga and compensating refund on every merchant rejection. Ask for evidence: Are there three independently deployed teams, divergent scaling needs, latency/availability targets, verified provider APIs, durable transactional boundaries, business-approved cancellation/refund policies, and fault injection showing a coordination requirement? If not, replacing local function calls with remote calls creates uncertain outcomes and operational burden without resolving the business decisions.

The current single-deployable, isolated composition is a justified intermediate step: it makes policies, authority, state-space interactions and failure questions visible. Its deterministic payment double is a **teaching port**, not an external abstraction guaranteeing eventual payment success. A future modular monolith may separate ownership internally; a later migration to distributed services requires a measured force and an explicit failure/consistency design. The next lesson will inspect boundaries—but this lesson does not begin that work.

Defend three propositions with evidence. First, independent states can coexist without a universal global status. Second, local atomic session updates preserve no-op behavior only in one JVM and cannot establish remote commit. Third, compensation after a capture cannot rewrite original financial history. If you cannot state which parts are proven versus hypothetical, the design is not ready for production claims.

## Retain — Transfer the composition mechanism to other domains

**Hotel reservation:** room allocation, guest payment, confirmation and housekeeping are distinct lifecycles. Merchant-first resembles reserving before charging; payment-first resembles prepaid booking. A booking cancellation after housekeeping work or a non-refundable cutoff needs contractual rules, not a universal state reset. A card authorization with no hotel confirmation resembles an accepted/paid mismatch.

**Banking transfer:** initiating a transfer, compliance approval, ledger posting, settlement and customer notification have separate owners. A local “submitted” state must not be mistaken for settled funds. A transfer reversal or return is an additional operation with unique identity and source evidence. Source-of-truth and unknown-outcome recovery remain the same conceptual challenges.

**Inventory and shipping:** reservation, picking, courier handoff, tracking and return each have a lifecycle. “Item shipped” cannot be rolled back to “never shipped” after carrier custody. A correction, recall or return is forward remediation. Use independent state product reasoning to find the cross-guards and the evidence missing from a global \`FULFILLED\` label.

For any unfamiliar domain, list entities, actor authorities, source evidence, policy alternatives, small state graphs, cross-state guards, partial states, irreversible effects, operational escalation, testable local hypotheses, and future remote experiments.
